The best wine grown in Georgia

Ten bottles that show what the state does well. High-elevation reds from the Dahlonega Plateau, crisp mountain whites from the Hiwassee valley, and the native muscadine that has grown in Georgia longer than anyone has been making wine from it.

The list

Our picks for the wines that best represent Georgia right now, chosen by grape and style. Each winery pours these regularly at its tasting room.

Picks are editorial and grouped by style, not by vintage. Releases change year to year, so check with each winery for what is pouring now. Please drink responsibly; 21+.

Three places to know

Georgia has two federally recognized wine regions in the mountains, plus a long tradition of muscadine growing across the south of the state.

American Viticultural Area
2018

Dahlonega Plateau

Georgia's first AVA entirely within the state. A rolling upland in Lumpkin and White counties where cool nights and well-drained slopes suit Cabernet Franc, Tannat and Petit Manseng. The densest cluster of tasting rooms in the state.

American Viticultural Area
2014

Upper Hiwassee Highlands

A higher, cooler valley region shared with North Carolina, around Blairsville, Hiawassee and Young Harris. Hybrids and aromatic whites do well here, along with lakeside tasting rooms.

Native grape country
Vitis

South Georgia & the Coastal Plain

Heat and humidity that defeat European vines suit the native muscadine. Expect bronze and black muscadine wines that range from bone dry to lush and sweet.

Grapes to know

The varieties that keep showing up on Georgia's best lists, and what to expect in the glass.

Petit MansengWhite · Southwest France

Thick skins shrug off Southern humidity. Tropical fruit with bright acidity, made dry or off-dry.

Norton / CynthianaRed · Native American

A homegrown hybrid of the Southeast. Dark berries, plum, a firm acidic spine. Ages better than you expect.

TannatRed · Southwest France

Big tannin and deep color. Mountain sites in Rabun County give it a lifted, peppery edge.

Cabernet FrancRed · Bordeaux

Ripens earlier than Cabernet Sauvignon, which matters before autumn rains. Red fruit, herbs, graphite.

MuscadineRed & bronze · Native

Vitis rotundifolia, the native grape of the Southeast. Musky, floral and grapey. Scuppernong is its best-known bronze form.

A weekend in the hills

A two-day loop from Atlanta. About 90 minutes to the first pour, with each day kept to three tasting rooms so the drive stays easy.

Day 1 · Morning

Dahlonega

Start at Three Sisters for Cynthiana, then Wolf Mountain for sparkling and lunch. Finish at Frogtown with a red blend on the deck.

Day 1 · Evening

Cleveland & Helen

Yonah Mountain's cave tour, then dinner in Helen and a stop at Habersham, one of Georgia's oldest wineries, for muscadine.

Day 2

Tiger, Rabun County

Tiger Mountain for Petit Manseng, Stonewall Creek for Tannat. Drive home through Tallulah Gorge.
